Risk-Aware Capacity Planning and Phase Change Material Storage for Fully Renewable Islanded Microgrids (Title)
Abstract
Diesel generators power most remote and mission-critical facilities because they are dispatchable and familiar, but they depend on fuel supply chains that are costly, polluting, and vulnerable to disruption. Fully renewable islanded microgrids remove that dependence yet face a reliability–cost dilemma: too little capacity causes service failures, while overbuilding makes the system unaffordable. This study asks whether two approaches — risk-aware capacity planning and phase-change-material (PCM) thermal energy storage — can ease this dilemma for a microgrid serving a forward operating base. A solar–battery–heat-pump system and an otherwise identical system with added hot and cold PCM storage are each sized three ways: two deterministic benchmarks and a conditional-value-at-risk (CVaR) optimization that explicitly weights adverse weather years. Every design is then tested on 25 years of unseen weather and load across five climate zones, under a range of reliability valuations, and benchmarked against diesel on total cost and the breakeven delivered fuel price. Loss of load is almost entirely thermal. Across the 25 unseen weather years, the CVaR design is the one that keeps its planning promise: the reliability it delivers out of sample matches what it was sized for (median test-to-plan unmet ratio ≈ 1.0), whereas the average-year design under-provisions and the worst-year design over-provisions. Against both deterministic benchmarks and a risk-neutral stochastic baseline, CVaR lowers the reliability tail and year-to-year cost variability, at a total-cost premium under 1.5% over the cheapest (average-year) design. Adding PCM lowers system cost by 4–14%, and the renewable microgrid beats diesel wherever delivered fuel exceeds roughly $9/gal — far below in-theater resupply costs. The results indicate when fully renewable islanded microgrids can be reliable enough to replace diesel and affordable enough to deploy. 
Keywords: capacity planning; islanded microgrid; phase-change material; thermal energy storage; stochastic optimization; conditional value-at-risk; loss of load; diesel replacement
